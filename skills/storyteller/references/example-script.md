# Umbrella 112

- **Project:** example
- **Script no.:** 001
- **Date:** 2026-10-28
- **Type:** Fiction
- **Language:** English
- **Format:** vertical, 6 clips x 8 s = about 48 s
- **Profile:** generic

## Angle

**Promise:** A night-shift lost-property clerk finds that the same umbrella is "claimed" every Tuesday by
someone who can't be seen, and that refusing to sign for it costs her more than letting it go.

**Why it's uncommon:** The haunting lives in paperwork, not a place: a ledger, a rule ("nothing leaves without
a signature") and a number. The horror is bureaucratic and quiet, and the rule is the weapon the clerk uses.
The count in the ledger (three hundred and twelve weekly claims) matches the six years since the owner died,
so the numbers are part of the clue.

**Obvious versions rejected:**
- A haunted bus station where passengers vanish.
- A ghost who comes back for a lost belonging.
- A night guard sees a figure on the CCTV.
- A cursed object that follows its finder home.
- A narrator who turns out to have been dead all along.

## Look anchors

- **Marisol:** a woman in her fifties in a grey cardigan, reading glasses on a beaded chain, short silver-streaked hair.
- **The counter:** a lost-property counter in an empty night bus terminal, green-tinted fluorescent light, steel shelves of numbered umbrellas, a brown cloth ledger open on the counter.
- **Umbrella 112:** a black umbrella with a worn wooden crook handle and a yellow paper tag tied on with string, hanging on a steel hook.

## Script

### Clip 01 — HOOK (8s)
- **Voiceover:** Every Tuesday at ten past four, someone collects umbrella 112. Its owner died six years ago.
- **Visual:** Low-angle locked-off shot of umbrella 112, a black umbrella with a worn wooden crook handle and a yellow paper tag tied on with string, hanging on a steel hook and swaying slightly though the air is still; green-tinted fluorescent light, rain streaking the dark glass behind it; very slow push-in. No subtitles, no on-screen text, no watermark, no logos.
- **Sound:** Low electrical hum, distant rain, no music.
- **Beat:** Opens the loop with a broken rule: a dead man's umbrella keeps being claimed.

### Clip 02 — SETUP (8s)
- **Voiceover:** I run lost property at the night terminal. Nothing leaves without a signature.
- **Visual:** Wide handheld shot of Marisol, a woman in her fifties in a grey cardigan with reading glasses on a beaded chain and short silver-streaked hair, behind a lost-property counter in an empty night bus terminal, steel shelves of numbered umbrellas behind her, squaring a brown cloth ledger on the counter with both hands; green-tinted fluorescent light. No subtitles, no on-screen text, no watermark, no logos.
- **Sound:** The hum continues; the soft slap of the ledger cover closing.
- **Beat:** Establishes the governing rule that the clerk will use against the haunting.

### Clip 03 — PATTERN (8s)
- **Voiceover:** Three hundred and twelve claims, one a week. The camera never catches a hand.
- **Visual:** Overhead shot of the brown cloth ledger on the counter, Marisol's thumb running down a long column of handwritten lines in soft focus and stopping at the last one, a small security monitor at the edge of frame showing the empty counter in grainy black and white; green-tinted fluorescent light. No subtitles, no on-screen text, no watermark, no logos.
- **Sound:** A dry paper rasp; the hum drops away for a beat.
- **Beat:** Turns the hook into evidence; 312 weekly claims is exactly six years, so the number is a clue.

### Clip 04 — TURN (8s)
- **Voiceover:** At four o'clock a name appears in the ledger by itself. Mr. Alcántara. My oldest regular.
- **Visual:** Close-up on the open ledger with a fountain pen lying untouched beside it while a line of looping blue-black ink writes itself across the page in soft focus; Marisol's reflection in the dark glass behind, motionless, one hand over her mouth; green-tinted fluorescent light. No subtitles, no on-screen text, no watermark, no logos.
- **Sound:** The scratch of a pen nib with no hand; a single low note.
- **Beat:** The turn: the claim is not a theft, it is a summons, and this week it is for someone she knows.

### Clip 05 — ESCALATION (8s)
- **Voiceover:** No signature, no exit. So I never signed. I held the hook with both hands.
- **Visual:** Medium shot from behind Marisol's shoulder as she grips the worn wooden handle of umbrella 112 on its steel hook with both hands, knuckles pale, while the umbrella shivers on the hook as if pulled toward the dark glass doors; green-tinted fluorescent light, a faint cold draught lifting the yellow tag. No subtitles, no on-screen text, no watermark, no logos.
- **Sound:** The hum rises into a strained whine; her breathing.
- **Beat:** She turns the rule into an act: she pays the cost of refusing.

### Clip 06 — PAYOFF (8s)
- **Voiceover:** Mr. Alcántara rode home safe. Umbrella 113 hangs on my hook now. Tagged with my name.
- **Visual:** Locked-off wide shot at dawn: Marisol seated behind the counter with her hands folded on the closed ledger, looking at a second black umbrella with a worn wooden crook handle hanging beside the first on the steel hook, its yellow paper tag turning slowly in the draught; pale grey light through the glass doors; very slow pull-back. No subtitles, no on-screen text, no watermark, no logos.
- **Sound:** Silence, then the faint tick of the tag against the hook.
- **Beat:** Callback: 112 becomes 113. Her refusal saved him and made her the next name.

## Full narration

Every Tuesday at ten past four, someone collects umbrella 112. Its owner died six years ago. I run lost property at the night terminal. Nothing leaves without a signature. Three hundred and twelve claims, one a week. The camera never catches a hand. At four o'clock a name appears in the ledger by itself. Mr. Alcántara. My oldest regular. No signature, no exit. So I never signed. I held the hook with both hands. Mr. Alcántara rode home safe. Umbrella 113 hangs on my hook now. Tagged with my name.

## Quality notes

- Hook 5: a broken rule with two numbers that can't both be true.
- Uncommonness 4: paperwork as the haunting, with the rule as the weapon; the "someone must pay" shape is familiar, the ledger is not.
- Clarity 4: one rule, one object, one turn.
- Payoff 4: the callback changes the meaning of the first line.
- Voice 4: short, flat, factual lines.
- The ending leaves the clerk alive; it avoids the "narrator was dead all along" twist.
