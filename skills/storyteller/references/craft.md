# Storyteller craft guide

Read this before writing the first script in a conversation. `SKILL.md` says what to do in what order;
this file says how to make the result detailed, on point, uncommon and interesting.

The four words, as tests a script has to pass:

- **Detailed** — every clip is fully specified: the line, the picture, the sound, and the new thing the viewer
  learns. Nobody has to guess what to generate or record.
- **On point** — every line serves the one-sentence promise. A clip that could be deleted without confusing
  anyone gets deleted.
- **Uncommon** — the viewer has not seen this version. Not a remix of the obvious one.
- **Interesting** — in every clip there is a reason not to swipe: a new fact, a turn, a question, an image.

## A. Finding an uncommon angle

Most scripts are forgettable because the first idea that arrives is the first idea everyone has. So the work
is to arrive at the tenth idea on purpose.

1. **Write the obvious set.** One line each for the five most obvious videos anyone would make from this topic
   or premise. These are banned, and so are small remixes of them (same story, new monster; same product
   demo, new hook).
2. **Generate at least eight candidate angles**, one line each, by pulling these levers:
   - *Specific over general*: a named kind of place, an object with a number on it, a date, a rule, a quantity.
     "A lost-property clerk at a night bus terminal" beats "a haunted bus station".
   - *Wrong way in*: enter through the object, the minor witness, the paperwork, the day after, the person who
     only heard about it.
   - *Governing rule*: invent or find a rule the world of the story obeys and let it drive every beat
     (nothing leaves without a signature; never count the bells). Reveal the rule a piece at a time.
   - *Inversion*: the opposite of what the viewer assumes ("the safest room in the house is where it happens").
   - *Collision*: put two unrelated worlds together (horror + bureaucracy, a product + a grandparent's habit).
   - *Odd true detail as the engine*: find a real, strange, checkable detail and build everything on it.
   - *Cost*: what does the character or viewer have to give up for the payoff? Stories with a price stay.
   - *Constraint*: a time-box or a limited view (one room, one night, one object, one phone call).
3. **Kill pass.** Discard an angle if (a) swapping the topic's name for another topic leaves the video
   unchanged, (b) it has no concrete detail yet, (c) the twist is guessable by clip 2, or (d) it is one of the
   obvious five in a costume.
4. **Choose** the angle with the strongest concrete detail and a clear turn, and write the **promise** in one
   sentence: who or what, the specific strange thing, the consequence, and the unusual way in.
5. **Stress test**: finish the sentence "By the end, the viewer knows / feels ___ that they didn't at the
   start." If you can't, the angle is a mood, not a story.

Keep the obvious set and the rejected angles in the script's Angle section. That record is what makes the
choice checkable.

## B. The hook

The first sentence and the first frame are one decision: they must both be strange, specific and unfinished.

Hook shapes that avoid the cliché openers:

- *The broken rule*: state a rule, then the thing that broke it.
- *The impossible specific*: two facts that can't both be true, with numbers.
- *Consequence before cause*: start at the result; the video is the "how".
- *The wrong-sized detail*: a tiny object with enormous consequences.
- *Mid-action*: the first line lands in the middle of something already happening.
- *The belief reversed*: a common belief, then one fact that breaks it.
- *The number that shouldn't be there*.

Rules: the first sentence is short (the profile sets a word limit); it names a concrete thing, not a feeling;
it opens a question the video does not answer until late; and frame 1 already contains the strangest visible
thing, with no empty establishing shot.

## C. Beats and retention

- **One idea per clip.** Each clip adds information or changes the situation. Restating is padding.
- **Open the loop by clip 2** and close it in the last or second-to-last clip. Put smaller open questions
  between, so there is always something unanswered.
- **Escalate along one axis** (stakes, proximity, certainty, or cost). Don't zigzag between them.
- **The turn** lands at about 60-70% of the runtime: a fact that changes the meaning of earlier clips. Plant
  it earlier in plain sight so the turn feels earned, not cheated.
- **The ending** is an image or a line, not a summary, a moral or "so remember to...". A deliberate echo of
  the hook is the one repeat that is allowed, and it is the strongest way to end.
- **Show, don't label.** Never tell the viewer the feeling (scary, shocking, amazing); give the detail that
  produces it.
- **Vary the rhythm**: a long clip of build-up, then a very short line. Silence is a beat: a clip with one
  sentence and a long hold can be the best clip.

## D. Line craft

- Written for the ear. Mostly short sentences, fragments allowed, one breath each. Read each line aloud in
  your head at the profile's pace.
- Concrete nouns and strong verbs; at most one adjective on a noun; no stacked intensifiers.
- Numbers as they are spoken ("ten past four", not "04:10").
- **No repeated words**: not twice in a row, not twice in one line, not the same two-word phrase in two
  clips. Say the same thing a different way or with a different image. One deliberate callback is allowed
  and should be marked in the clip's Beat.
- **Swap test**: could this line be moved into a different video unchanged? If yes, it is generic; rewrite it
  with a detail only this story has.
- **So-what test**: if the viewer shrugs at the line, cut it or give it a consequence.
- Write in the profile's language as people speak it. For Taglish, code-switch the way a real speaker does,
  not by translating sentence by sentence.

## E. Facts and honesty

- **Fiction** is labelled Fiction. If it uses real folklore, custom or history, say in Notes which parts are
  traditional and which you invented.
- **Non-fiction** (history, science, true events, how-things-work): search first, build on checkable specifics,
  and list every checkable claim in the Fact sheet with its source URL. Never invent a statistic, date, quote,
  name or event. If a surprising claim has only one source, say so in the Fact sheet or cut it.
- **Real people**: no invented quotes, no accusations; keep private individuals out.
- **Ads**: only claims that come from the product listing or from the owner. No guaranteed results, medical or
  income claims, fake testimonials or reviews, or unbacked superlatives. If a claim can't be sourced, it
  doesn't go in the script.
- When you aren't sure a fact is true, say that in the Notes. A script with one honest "unverified" is better
  than a smooth lie.

## F. Visual prompts for Google Flow (Veo)

Each clip is one prompt for one continuous shot of a few seconds.

- Order the prompt: subject → action → setting → light → camera move → mood. One main action per clip.
- Be physical and filmable. "A woman's thumb traces down a ledger column and stops" is a shot; "tension
  builds" is not.
- **Look anchors**: define each recurring character, place and product once in the Look anchors section and
  paste that descriptor word for word into every visual prompt where it appears. This is what keeps faces,
  clothes and products from drifting between clips.
- Vary the framing and camera move between neighbouring clips (a slow push-in, a locked-off wide, a handheld
  follow, a low angle, an over-the-shoulder, a close detail), because repeated shots read as the same clip.
- Avoid what AI video does badly: readable text or numbers, many hands counting or doing fiddly work, crowd
  faces, fast complex hand actions, exact lip-sync, objects that must keep an exact shape through a move. Cut
  away to a detail or frame from behind instead.
- Obey the profile's visual rules, including what must end each prompt.

## G. Review before the lint

Score the draft honestly on five points, from 1 to 5: **hook**, **uncommonness**, **clarity**, **payoff** and
**voice**. Rewrite anything below 4. If the uncommonness score is low, go back to step A.3 rather than polish.
Record the one-line reason for each score in Quality notes.

## H. Clichés to cut on sight

- Openers: "Did you know", "In a world where", "Let's dive in", "Welcome back", "What if I told you",
  "You won't believe", "In today's video".
- Closers: "Stay tuned", "Without further ado", a moral epilogue, a recap of what we just saw.
- Filler words: "literally", "game-changer", "unbelievable", "mind-blowing".
- Plot crutches: "it was all a dream", the narrator was dead all along (unless the whole story is built to
  earn it), a character explaining the rule that the story should have shown.
